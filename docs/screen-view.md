# Screen view from the phone

A live view of what is on the PC's screen (issue #1520) that you can also click, drag, scroll and type into (issue #1521, **Control** below). Tap the CPU reading in any page header to open the **PC load** sheet, then its **Screen** row. On the phone the view opens full screen. On a desktop browser the same row pops the view out into its own window (`/?screen=1`, which boots only the viewer). It needs no extra app and no admin rights: the tray-owned webapp runs in your interactive session, so it reads the desktop directly with plain GDI.

- **Displays.** With more than one monitor, a picker in the bar names them by where they sit: **Main** for the primary, then **Above**, **Below**, **Left** or **Right**. It opens on the last monitor you picked on this device (issue #1603), kept in the browser as `launcher.screenMonitor` with its position on the desktop, so it survives a reload, the pop-out window and a restarted app; if that monitor is gone it opens on the primary without a message. Nothing is stored with a single monitor. Monitors use virtual-desktop coordinates, so one above or left of the primary (a negative origin) works like any other.
- **Zoom.** Pinch to zoom (up to 5×) and drag to pan. On a desktop, scroll the wheel to zoom and double-click to reset. The zoom is local to the view, since the page itself stays zoom-locked.
- **Landscape (issue #1601).** On a phone held sideways (a touch screen in a window under 500 px tall) the title bar and the View / Control row leave the layout and the picture takes their height. A pill at the top left corner of the picture names the current mode, **View** or **Control**, so the hidden switch hides no state. Tap it and the bar and the switches come down over the picture, which does not move or re-fit; tap it again, tap the picture (that tap only closes the bars, it never clicks the PC), pick a display, or raise the keyboard, and they go away. The mode and style switches stay up, so you can change mode and then reach what it reveals. Turning the phone upright puts the bars back in place, and the desktop pop-out window and tablets never hide them. The picture and the keys bar clear a notch by the left and right safe-area insets.
- **Cursor.** The PC's mouse cursor is drawn over the picture (issue #1598), in its current shape: arrow, text caret, hand, busy, the four resize arrows, move and not-allowed; any other cursor draws as the arrow. It follows the real mouse even when nothing else on screen changes, stays on its spot and keeps its size while you zoom and pan, and hides when it is on another monitor, panned out of view, or hidden on the PC. It never takes a touch, so the view's gestures work as before. In Control, a tap moves the real cursor there, so the drawn cursor jumping to your finger confirms where the click landed.
- **States.** A locked or asleep PC can't be read from a user-session process (the lock screen is Windows' secure desktop). The view says so: the last frame stays under a "Last updated … · live data unavailable" line, or, with no frame yet, a message that the screen may be locked or asleep. It picks up again by itself when the desktop comes back. A refusal names its cause (Tailscale-only, passkey, display gone) and never retries.
- **Reconnecting.** A dropped connection (a Wi-Fi gap, the phone changing network) reconnects by itself, with the last frame left under a quiet *Reconnecting…* line (issue #1603). The waits grow 1 s, 2 s, 4 s, 8 s, then 15 s, and a drawn frame resets them; after seven attempts, about a minute, it gives up into the **Retry** card. Each attempt is a fresh connection through the same gate as the first (the passkey token is asked for again, Tailscale only, the tunnel refused), and a refusal ends the loop. Nothing retries while the page is in the background (coming back reconnects) or after the view is closed, so the PC never captures for nobody. Input stays off until the new connection has drawn its first frame, and a drag in progress at the drop is forgotten rather than released into the new connection (the PC already let go of the button when the old one closed). A reconnect shows in the audit log as an ordinary `screen_close` / `screen_open` pair; the attempt number and wait are logged on the phone's console only.
- **Quality and readout (issue #1721).** A sliders button at the right end of the row under the bar opens a small **Quality** sheet with three presets and a **Readout** switch. **Sharp** is JPEG quality 85 at the phone's width, **Balanced** (the default) is quality 70, the same width and about 7 frames a second, exactly as before presets, and **Data saver** is at most 960 px wide, quality 50 and about 3 frames a second. The choice is remembered per device (`launcher.screenPreset`, like the monitor), rides the connect query as `preset=` and is applied on the next open and on every reconnect; picking another one reconnects once, with the last frame left under a quiet *Changing the quality…* line. The server owns what each preset means (one table in `src/desktop_capture.py`, tests pin the numbers) and treats an unknown value as Balanced. The **Readout** puts a small overlay over the top left of the picture: frames a second, kB a second (1 kB is 1000 bytes), the average frame size and the time from the phone confirming a frame to the next one arriving, all worked out from the frames the page actually received over the last 5 seconds, plus the PC's own times for grab, digest, resize and encode in milliseconds. Switching it on sends one small `{"type": "readout", "on": true}` message (it is not input and works in View too); the server then sends a `stats` message about once a second, and with the readout off it sends none. The readout is off every time the view opens. Those PC times are the measurement for judging the capture path.

**Same gate as the terminal.** A picture of the desktop shows whatever is on it, terminals included, so `/api/screen/*` is terminal-grade (#1036): refused over the Cloudflare tunnel, Tailscale-only, bearer token, and a passkey unlock. `GET /api/screen/monitors` gets that from its `_TERMINAL_GUARD_RULES` row. The `WS /api/screen/ws` stream re-applies the same legs inline, because the HTTP middleware never sees a WebSocket handshake (`app/webapp/routers/desktop_screen.py`). The PC itself (loopback) bypasses, as it does for the terminal.

**How it streams** (`src/desktop_capture.py`):

- The process is set per-monitor-v2 DPI aware before the first capture, so frames and monitor rects are in physical pixels.
- Each grab BitBlts only the chosen monitor's rect. Pillow's `ImageGrab` grabs the whole virtual desktop and crops, which is why the capture uses BitBlt instead.
- The frame is downscaled to the width the viewer asks for (at most 1920 from the phone, 960 on Data saver) and JPEG-encoded at the preset's quality.
- **An unchanged screen sends nothing.** A digest of a 4×-reduced thumbnail decides, so an idle desktop costs one frame.
- **Flow control is client-acked.** The next frame is grabbed only after the viewer acks the last one it drew, at most one every 150 ms (~7 fps; 300 ms on Data saver), so a slow link never builds a queue.
- **Capture runs only while a viewer is connected.** There is no background task. Closing the view, or hiding the page, closes the socket, and the loop stops with it.
- **The cursor has its own messages** (`src/desktop_cursor.py`). The capture never contains the cursor, and a cursor-only move changes no frame, so a second loop on the same socket polls `GetCursorInfo` about 30 times a second and sends `{"type": "cursor", "x", "y", "shape", "visible"}` (position as 0-1 in the shown monitor) only when it changed. It is not paced by the frame ack, and it stops with the socket like the frames. Only a shape name goes out, matched against Windows' standard cursors, never the cursor's image, since an app's cursor can carry its content. While the cursor can't be read (the lock screen), it is sent hidden once, logged once, and retried once a second. Cursor messages are not logged.

Every connect and disconnect writes one line to `webapp/terminal_audit.log` (`[screen_open]` / `[screen_close]`, with client IP, monitor, the quality preset (on open), duration, frames sent and, on close, the bytes sent, how many times the desktop stopped being readable as `unavailable` (the one lasting trace of a blocked capture), the number of input messages acted on, and `clipboard_to_pc`, how many clipboard transfers to the PC went through: a count of transfers, never what they carried or how long it was). The close line is written even when the handler is cancelled mid-teardown (#1688). What was typed is never logged.

## Controlling the PC

A **View / Control** switch sits under the Screen bar. **View** is the default every time the view opens, so a stray tap never clicks: touches only zoom and pan the picture, as above. **Control** sends what you do to the PC:

- **Tap** clicks there. Two quick taps double-click, on the first tap's spot.
- **Long-press** (half a second) right-clicks.
- **Press and drag** drags with the left button held (move a window, select text).
- **Magnifier loupe** (issue #1725): while one finger is down in Touch, after a moment, a round loupe above it shows the picture under the finger at 2.5x, so you can aim at a small target the finger covers. It follows the finger as it drags and goes on lift, in View, in Trackpad, with two fingers down and while the picture is not live. It only draws what the phone already has and never takes a touch: the click, long-press and drag points are the same with or without it.
- **Two-finger drag** scrolls the wheel, in the phone's own direction (fingers down shows what is above). Sideways scrolls horizontally.
- **Two-finger tap** (both fingers down and up together, under half a second, without moving) right-clicks at the point between the fingers, with no left click. Resting the two fingers longer, or moving them, is not a tap.
- **Three-finger tap** (the same quick tap with three fingers) raises the keyboard and keys bar, or lowers them again. Three fingers never click, drag or scroll, and in View they do nothing.
- **Pinch** still zooms, and moving the pinch pans, which is how you move around a zoomed picture in Control.
- On a desktop browser the mouse works the same way. Right-click right-clicks, and the wheel scrolls the PC (ctrl+wheel or a trackpad pinch zooms).

**Touch or Trackpad.** Once Control is on, a second switch beside it picks how one finger drives the PC. **Touch** is the list above: the tap point is the click point, which is quick for big targets. **Trackpad** works like a laptop's trackpad, so your finger never covers what it aims at:

The gestures follow the Windows remote desktop app's mouse-pointer mode. Touching never clicks by itself:

- **One-finger drag**, anywhere on the picture, moves the cursor by how far the finger travels, not to where it lands, and never clicks, however long the finger rested first. Slow is precise, a quick flick crosses the screen.
- **Tap** (touch and lift without moving) clicks at the cursor. Two quick taps double-click there. A single tap's click waits a quarter second for a possible second tap.
- **Long-press** without moving (half a second, a ring appears round the cursor), then lift: a right-click at the cursor. A **two-finger tap** right-clicks at the cursor too, and a **three-finger tap** raises or lowers the keyboard, as in Touch.
- **Double-tap and hold, then drag**: a drag with the left button held (move a window, select text), released when you lift.
- **Two-finger drag** scrolls at the cursor, and **pinch** zooms as in Touch. While zoomed in, the picture pans to keep the cursor in view.
- The cursor starts on the PC's own, or mid-picture when the PC's is on another display. Move the real mouse and the next drag starts from there.

The style is **remembered per device**: picking Control uses whichever you used last on this phone or browser, Touch the first time (or when the browser blocks storage). The view itself still opens in View every time.

**Keyboard.** In Control, either style, the keyboard button raises the phone keyboard and shows a keys bar. Whatever you type goes to the PC verbatim, accents and symbols included (`ñ€` arrive as typed, sent as Unicode). Backspace and Return work from the phone keyboard. The bar adds what a phone keyboard lacks: Esc, Tab, Del, the arrows, Backspace and Enter, plus **Ctrl, Alt, Shift and Win**. The modifiers are sticky for one key: tap Ctrl, then `c`, for Ctrl+C. Several can be held together (Ctrl, Shift, then Esc). A held modifier lights up and lets go after the next key, so a forgotten Ctrl never turns later typing into shortcuts. Combos go out as real key presses, so apps see Ctrl+C as a shortcut, not as a typed character. A modifier applies to any printable ASCII character, not only letters and digits: Ctrl then `/` is Ctrl+/ (#1602). The PC resolves the character on the keyboard layout it has in the foreground, so a character that needs Shift there (`?` on a US layout) goes out with the Shift it needs, once, even if you also held Shift; a character that needs no Shift never gains one. A character the layout can only type with AltGr, or not at all (`@` on a Spanish layout), is refused rather than guessed, and an accent or emoji with a modifier is still typed as text. **Task Mgr** sends Ctrl+Shift+Esc as one combo: Ctrl+Alt+Del is the Windows secure attention sequence, which no app can send, so this key is the stand-in. Whether it opens Task Manager on a given PC depends on the limit below (Task Manager can run elevated). The key's first live check is pending. A second page of keys sits behind **F-keys ›**: F1 to F12 and Home, End, PgUp and PgDn, with **‹ Keys** back. A modifier tapped on either page is still held on the other (the way back names the ones held) and lets go after the next key, so Ctrl then F5 is Ctrl+F5 and Alt then F4 is Alt+F4. The bar opens on the first page every time. In a landscape phone the same keys lay out on two rows instead of four. The last row of each page holds **Paste to PC** and **Paste + Ctrl+V**, see "Clipboard" below.

**Hardware keyboard.** In Control with the view open, a keyboard attached to the iPad (or the desktop pop-out window's own keyboard) drives the PC directly, with no keys bar needed (#1723). Arrows, Esc, Tab, Enter, Backspace, Delete, Home, End, PgUp, PgDn and F1 to F12 go out as those keys, and Ctrl, Alt or Shift held with a letter, digit, space or printable character goes out as one combo, so Ctrl+C, Ctrl+V and Alt+F4 act on the PC. **Cmd is sent as Ctrl** (Cmd+C is Ctrl+C on the PC), never as Win, since Windows has no Cmd; Win stays on the bar. Plain typing still goes through the phone-keyboard path, so a character is never sent twice, and a modifier lit on the bar combines with a hardware key and lets go after it. A key that was sent is kept from the browser (F5 does not reload the launcher, Esc does not close the view while you are in Control); nothing is sent, or kept from the browser, in View, while the picture is stale, or during an IME composition. A held key repeats, at a steady pace and up to a cap. Known limit: iPadOS and the browser keep some shortcuts for themselves (Cmd+Tab, Cmd+Space, the Globe key, Ctrl+W / Ctrl+T), and a key the page never receives cannot be forwarded. Plain text typed with the keys bar closed has nowhere to go, since the hidden input has no focus: tap the keyboard button first. Logs record that input happened, never which keys.

**Clipboard (Paste to PC).** Text you copied on the phone can go onto the PC's clipboard. **Paste to PC** reads the phone's clipboard when you tap it (iOS shows its own *Paste* confirmation, which is the phone asking you, not the launcher) and puts that text on the PC's clipboard, replacing what was there; a short line over the picture says *Sent to the PC clipboard*. **Paste + Ctrl+V** does the same and then presses Ctrl+V on the PC, but only after the PC confirms the copy landed, so a failed copy never pastes what the PC's clipboard held before. If the phone refuses to let the page read its clipboard, a small sheet opens where you paste the text by hand and send it. Text only, up to 64 KB of it (its own limit, apart from the 1000 characters a typed message carries); an empty clipboard or a longer text says so and sends nothing, and a PC clipboard that another program holds locked is retried for a moment and then reports *busy* in its own words. It works like any other input: only in Control, only while the picture is live, over the same gated socket, and the keys are greyed out otherwise. Logs record only that a transfer happened and its direction (`clipboard_to_pc` on the close line), never the text, never its length. The other direction, copying from the PC to the phone, is a separate step.

**Input is never sent at a stale picture.** While the screen can't be read (locked, asleep, disconnected) the view sends nothing, since a tap would land on whatever is really there now.

**Same socket, same gate.** Input travels as JSON messages on the Screen view's own `WS /api/screen/ws`, so it has the same edge / Tailscale / bearer / passkey gate as the picture, and there is no other input route. `tests/test_desktop_input.py` fails if any other module calls a Windows input API, or if anything but the Screen socket imports the injector. Each message is checked (coordinates within 0-1, known keys and buttons, bounded text and scroll), and anything else is dropped. When the socket closes, for whatever reason, every mouse button and key still held on the PC is released, so a dropped phone mid-drag never leaves a button stuck.

**How it maps** (`src/desktop_input.py`). The phone sends positions as 0-1 within the monitor it shows. The server turns that into a physical pixel on that monitor, then into the 0-65535 range Windows `SendInput` takes over the whole virtual desktop (`MOUSEEVENTF_ABSOLUTE | MOUSEEVENTF_VIRTUALDESK`). Rects are physical pixels (the process is per-monitor-v2 DPI aware), so a scaled monitor, or one above or left of the primary, maps like any other. Text goes out as `KEYEVENTF_UNICODE` key packets, and named keys and modifiers as virtual keys. No extra tool, no admin rights: `SendInput` is reached through `ctypes`.

**Known limits.**

- **Elevated windows (UIPI).** The webapp runs without admin rights, and Windows doesn't let a non-elevated process send input to an elevated window (an admin terminal, Task Manager, an installer). The input is silently dropped, so the click does nothing. Not a bug; use the PC itself for those.
- **The lock screen, UAC prompts and the secure desktop** take no input from here, and the picture can't show them either.
- **No clipboard sync, file transfer or audio.** The clipboard goes one way, on a tap, as text: phone to PC (Paste to PC, above). Nothing is synced on its own, nothing is read from the PC's clipboard, and a transfer replaces whatever the PC's clipboard held.
- A long-press can't be told from a slow tap under half a second, so tap briskly for a left click.

**Tests never touch the real desktop or clipboard.** The clipboard has its own seam (`src/desktop_clipboard.py`), swapped for a recorder by the same switch, and `tests/test_desktop_clipboard.py` fails if a clipboard API appears anywhere but that module, or if it is reached from anywhere but the gated socket. Every event goes through one injector seam. The test suite swaps it for a recorder, and the pre-ship gate's disposable webapp starts with `LAUNCHER_SCREEN_FAKE_INPUT=1`, which records and sends nothing. The real check (taps on both monitors, the context menu, typing `ñ€ hola` into Notepad, Ctrl+A / Ctrl+C) is on the phone.

Capture and input live in the webapp process, so `tray.bat --restart` makes a change here live. Nothing here is part of the session-host.
